# Tailoring rules (from Nir's CV method)

## Why only the yellow areas
Nir built the skeleton — job headings, achievement bullets, metrics — with the
candidate, and the candidate can defend every line of it. The yellow areas are
the parts designed to flex per application. Keeping edits inside them means
the CV stays truthful, keeps its look, and stays one page.

## The golden rule — no invention
Use only what is in the CV or what the candidate told you in this
conversation. Never add a technology, tool, domain, number, or responsibility
because the job asks for it. If the job requires something the candidate
lacks, it goes in the "gaps" line of the final message, not in the CV.

Allowed moves:
- **Reorder** — put the job's most important matching skills first.
- **Swap spelling to the job's** — "Postgres" → "PostgreSQL" when the job
  writes it that way (same technology).
- **Swap in other real skills** — the CV elsewhere (bullets, skills,
  education) or the candidate's answer contains a skill that matches; bring it
  into the relevant yellow area and drop a less relevant one to keep length.
- **Choose the title** — the target-title line may use the job's title when
  it honestly describes the candidate (e.g. "Full Stack Developer" →
  "Backend Developer" for a backend-heavy profile). Don't raise seniority
  ("Senior", "Lead", "Manager") beyond what the CV supports. Drop "Junior" /
  "Entry level" from the job's title — it lowers the candidate for no gain.
  If the summary repeats the title (a yellow area at its start), change it
  to match the new title, unless that makes the sentence untrue.
- **Name real work in the job's words** — when a bullet or the candidate's
  answer describes an activity in plain words and the job has a standard
  term for exactly that activity, the term may go in a yellow area.
  Example: "wrote Python scripts that analyze test results automatically"
  supports "test automation" and "Python scripting". Not allowed: the job
  says "hardware bring-up" and the CV only says "fabricated and debugged a
  board" — close, but not the same claim; list it as a gap and let the
  candidate decide. Test: could the candidate describe, in an interview,
  a real case where they did exactly what the term says?

## Area by area
- **Target title** (under the name): match the job's title if truthful.
- **Keyword line** (`Skill | Skill | Skill ...`): must stay ONE line.
  It is bold and usually already fills the whole row, so keep its length
  **equal to or shorter than** the original — even +2 characters can push
  the last item onto a second line (seen in testing). Put the job's top
  matching skills first; drop the least relevant item to make room.
- **Summary phrases**: only the yellow phrases change; the sentence around
  them stays. Make sure the edited sentence still reads naturally.
- **`Focused on ...` lists** (one per major role): keywords that are true for
  THAT role — don't move a skill from one job into another job's list. Don't
  repeat something that a bullet in the same role already says.
- **Education / course `Focus on` lists**: good place for real keywords the
  job wants that the work experience doesn't show.
- **Professional skills values**: reorder so matching items come first;
  you may add a real skill and drop a weaker one. Category labels are not
  yellow and stay as they are; a skill with no perfect category goes into
  the closest one (Linux and Git → the programming/tools row). If it truly
  fits nowhere, leave it in the summary or Focused-on list and mention in
  the final message that the candidate can rename a category in Word.

## Length and layout
- Aim for the same character count as the original area or shorter — a few
  characters of growth at most. Areas in the same paragraph share one
  budget: in testing, two summary phrases that each grew +6 together added
  a fourth line to the summary. The `apply` command prints old vs new
  length for each area — check it.
- A line that used to fit on one row must still fit; a two-row item must not
  end with a dangling 1–3 word last row.
- The final page count must equal the original's. Verify, don't estimate.

## Language
- CV text: English, matching the job's terminology.
- Conversation with the candidate: Hebrew.
